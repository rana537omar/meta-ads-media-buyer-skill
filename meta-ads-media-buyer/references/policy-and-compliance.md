# Policy, ad review and account health

Meta's Advertising Standards are enforced mostly by automated systems, and they change. Use this file to spot likely problems and choose the compliant route. For anything decisive, confirm the current wording in Meta's Transparency Center and Business Help Center, and remind the user that local advertising and consumer law also applies.

## 1. Frequent reasons ads are rejected

- **Personal attributes**: copy that asserts or implies something about the viewer's health, finances, race, religion, age, sexual orientation, gender identity, disability, criminal record or name. "Are you struggling with debt?" is a classic trigger.
- **Health and appearance**: before-and-after images, close-ups of body parts, imagery that creates negative self-perception, promises of specific weight loss or cures.
- **Misleading or unsubstantiated claims**: guaranteed results, exaggerated earnings, "miracle" or "instant" outcomes, fake scarcity or countdowns.
- **Unacceptable business practices**: hidden terms, deceptive pricing, bait and switch.
- **Landing page problems**: page does not match the ad, does not work, forces downloads, has disruptive pop-ups, or lacks business and policy information.
- **Intellectual property**: other brands' logos, copyrighted music, footage or celebrity images without rights.
- **Facebook or Instagram brand misuse**: implying endorsement, altered logos.
- **Sensational, shocking or sexual content**; excessive profanity.
- **Circumvention**: obscured words, symbols replacing letters, cloaked destinations. This escalates from a rejected ad to a disabled account.
- **Missing authorisation or category declaration** for regulated topics.

## 2. Rewriting risky copy

Talk about the product and the outcome, not about the person. Make claims specific and provable.

| Risky | Safer |
|---|---|
| "Are you overweight and tired of diets?" | "A meal plan built around foods people actually enjoy." |
| "Struggling with bad credit?" | "Credit-building tools explained in plain language." |
| "Lose 10 kg in 2 weeks, guaranteed." | "A 12-week coaching programme with weekly check-ins." |
| "You have anxiety. We can fix it." | "Guided sessions designed to help people manage everyday stress." |
| "Earn 5,000 a month from home, easily!" | "Learn the skills freelancers use to find their first clients." |
| "Only 2 left!" (when untrue) | State real stock or a real deadline, or drop the urgency |

Softening a number with "up to" or a small-print disclaimer does not make it safe: "save up to 50%" style outcome claims are still commonly rejected and attract consumer-protection scrutiny, especially in finance and health. Prefer describing the service and process, or cite a documented typical result.

Keep testimonials genuine, typical and documented. Add required disclaimers for the sector and country.

## 3. Special ad categories

Declare the category at campaign level when ads concern:
- **Financial products and services** (credit, loans, insurance, and related offers)
- **Employment**
- **Housing**
- **Social issues, elections or politics** (also needs identity verification and a "paid for by" disclaimer in most countries)

Declared campaigns have limited targeting: typically no narrow age or gender selection, limited location precision, no standard lookalikes, and reduced detailed targeting. The exact restrictions depend on the country. Running such ads without declaring the category leads to rejection and repeated violations put the account at risk. Rely on creative and broad delivery to find the audience.

## 4. Restricted industries

These can advertise only under conditions such as age limits, country limits, licences or written permission from Meta: alcohol; online gambling and gaming; dating services; prescription drugs, online pharmacies and telehealth; over-the-counter medicines; weight loss and cosmetic procedures; financial and insurance services; cryptocurrency products; addiction treatment; CBD and hemp products; subscription services with negative-option billing.

Not allowed at all: illegal products, tobacco and vaping products, weapons and explosives, unsafe supplements, adult products and services (with narrow exceptions), payday-type loans, multi-level marketing income pitches, spyware, counterfeit goods, and discriminatory practices.

Check the current rule for the specific product and country before planning a campaign in these areas.

## 5. Handling a rejected ad

1. Read the stated policy reason in Account Quality.
2. Check the whole ad: text, image or video (including on-screen text and audio), headline, landing page, and the page name.
3. If the ad truly breaks a rule, edit it. Editing resubmits it for review.
4. If the rejection looks mistaken (automated review makes errors), request a review once, with a short factual explanation.
5. Do not resubmit the same rejected creative again and again, and do not duplicate it to slip through: accumulated rejections harm the account.
6. Keep a note of what was rejected and why, to avoid repeating it.

## 6. Handling a restricted account

1. Open Account Quality (Business Support Home) and see which asset is restricted: ad account, business portfolio, page, or personal profile, and the stated reason.
2. Complete any requested verification (identity, business verification, two-factor authentication).
3. Request a review, stating briefly what the business does, that it follows the policies, and what was corrected if a violation happened.
4. Use official support chat where it is offered.
5. While waiting, do not create new accounts or use someone else's to carry on. That is circumvention and often turns a recoverable restriction into a permanent one across all connected assets.
6. If the decision is final, the legitimate path is addressing the underlying cause; some regions also offer external appeal or dispute bodies.

## 7. Protecting the account

- Complete business verification; two-factor authentication for everyone with access.
- At least two trusted admins on the business portfolio; remove former staff and agencies promptly.
- Use partner access for agencies rather than sharing logins.
- Reliable payment method with a backup; pay balances on time.
- Keep page quality healthy: moderate comments, answer complaints, deliver what the ads promise (customer feedback scores affect delivery costs and can restrict advertising).
- Warm up new accounts with modest budgets and clean creative.
- Keep landing pages complete: contact details, privacy policy, terms, refund policy.

## 8. Requests to decline

Decline, and explain the risk and the compliant alternative, when asked to:
- Get around review or enforcement: cloaking, disguised words, rotating domains, new accounts after a ban, rented or bought accounts.
- Invent testimonials, reviews, endorsements, results, credentials or scarcity.
- Make claims the business cannot substantiate, or impersonate another brand or person.
- Target or exclude people in ways that discriminate in housing, employment or financial services.
- Upload purchased or scraped contact lists, or use personal data without consent.
- Promote prohibited products or scams.

The alternative usually exists: a truthful claim with real proof, the correct category and authorisation, a proper appeal, or first-party data collected with consent.
