## 1. Review rhythm

Checking too often leads to over-editing, which resets learning and makes results worse.

| When | What to do |
|---|---|
| Daily (5 minutes) | Spend pacing, delivery errors, rejected ads, broken links, comments needing moderation, anything wildly off. No strategic changes |
| Twice a week | Ad-level cut and keep decisions using the rules below |
| Weekly | Full funnel review against targets, budget shifts, creative results, next tests |
| Monthly | Structure, audience and offer review; MER and new-customer trends; creative learnings; plan |

Use at least the last 7 days for decisions, alongside 14 and 30 days for context. Use 3 days only as an early warning. Remember that the most recent days are under-reported when purchases lag clicks.

## 2. The learning phase

A new or significantly edited ad set explores before settling. Performance is less stable and often more expensive until it has roughly 50 optimisation events within 7 days.

Edits that usually restart learning: changing targeting, creative, optimisation event or bid strategy; adding a new ad; pausing for 7 days or more; large budget changes (small steps of up to about 20 percent generally do not).

Working rules:
- Batch edits and make them at one time rather than a little every day.
- Leave new ad sets alone for 3 to 5 days unless something is clearly broken or spending far above target with nothing to show.
- "Learning limited" means too few conversions for the setup. Fix it by consolidating ad sets, raising budget, widening the audience, or optimising for a more frequent event. An ad set that is learning limited and hitting its CPA target does not need fixing.

## 3. Cut, keep or scale

Adapt thresholds to the account. Expensive conversions need more spend before a verdict. T = target CPA.

**Ads**
| Situation | Action |
|---|---|
| Spent 2 to 3 x T with zero conversions | Pause |
| Spent 1 x T, zero conversions, and weak early signals (link CTR well under account average, low hook rate, high CPC) | Pause early |
| Spent 1 x T, zero conversions, but strong early signals | Let it reach 2 x T |
| CPA above 1.3 to 1.5 x T after 3 x T spend and several conversions | Pause, or move to retargeting only if it performs there |
| CPA within about 20 percent of T | Keep; watch the trend |
| CPA at least 20 percent better than T with 5 to 10 or more conversions, stable for several days | Winner: move to the scaling campaign and build variations |
| Gets almost no spend inside an ad set with winners | Normal. Leave it, or retest the concept in its own ad set if you believe in it |

**Ad sets and campaigns**
| Situation | Action |
|---|---|
| Hitting target for 5 to 7 days, not constrained by audience size | Scale (section 4) |
| Slightly above target, improving | Hold; add new creative |
| Above target for 7 days or more despite new creative | Cut budget or pause; revisit offer, landing page and tracking |
| Frequency high and rising, CTR falling | Creative fatigue or audience too small: new creative, or widen |

For ROAS goals use the same logic inverted, and prefer rules based on spend and number of purchases over ROAS from one or two orders.

Before cutting anything, check that the cause is not outside the ad: site outage, out-of-stock product, broken tracking, a holiday, or a price change.

## 4. Scaling methods

**Vertical: more budget on what works**
- Raise budgets about 10 to 20 percent every 2 to 3 days while CPA holds. Slow and steady keeps learning intact.
- Bigger jumps (50 to 100 percent) are reasonable when performance is far better than target and the business has stock and capacity; expect a few unstable days.
- Use cost per result goals or ROAS goals as guard rails on large budgets so extra spend only happens at acceptable cost.
- Scale at campaign-budget level in consolidated campaigns rather than nudging many ad sets.

**Horizontal: more surface for the same efficiency**
- New creative concepts and angles. This is the most dependable way to scale, because each distinct concept reaches different people.
- New formats of proven angles, new creators, partnership ads.
- New audiences where creative alone has plateaued: additional countries or regions, wider age ranges, lookalikes from better seeds.
- New offers, bundles and products to lift AOV and conversion rate, which raises the CPA the business can afford.
- New placements or languages that current assets do not serve well.

**Automation**
- Automated rules can protect the account: pause an ad that spends 3 x T with no conversions; cut budget if CPA exceeds a ceiling over 3 days; notify on a drop in events. Use rules for protection more than for decision-making.

## 5. When scaling breaks

Costs normally rise somewhat with spend, because the cheapest conversions are bought first. Work out with the user how much CPA increase is acceptable for the extra volume (marginal CPA matters more than average CPA).

If CPA climbs sharply after a budget increase:
1. Wait 3 to 4 days before reacting, unless the loss is severe.
2. If it does not recover, step back to the last stable budget rather than pausing.
3. Check frequency and reach: saturation calls for new creative or wider audiences, not more budget.
4. Check MER and total revenue. Platform CPA may look worse while the business is doing better, or the reverse.

Scaling limits are often outside the ad account: conversion rate, AOV, stock, fulfilment, sales team capacity, cash flow. Raise these when the numbers point to them.

## 6. Budget allocation

A reasonable starting split for an established account, to adjust with evidence:
- 70 to 80 percent proven prospecting (scaling campaign)
- 10 to 20 percent creative testing
- 5 to 15 percent retargeting and retention, sized to the warm audience rather than to its flattering ROAS

Move budget toward the best marginal return, and keep the testing budget alive even when the account is performing: today's tests are next month's winners.

## 7. Promotions and seasonal peaks

- Before: build warm audiences and test creative in the weeks leading up, so winners are known before costs rise. Prepare and submit promotional ads early because review slows at peak times.
- During: CPMs rise but conversion rates usually rise more. Scale budgets on proven ads, use scheduled or lifetime budgets for short windows, and raise or loosen cost caps so delivery is not choked.
- After: return budgets to normal promptly, switch off expired offers, and retarget new buyers with follow-up offers later.
- Compare results with the same period last year or the previous promotion, not with an ordinary week.
